NoteWhere the project stands

I’ve prepared the competitor comparison and research methods. The content audit, survey analysis, and final recommendations are still planned work.

What I’m Working On

What do prospective patients want to know before contacting PhD Orthodontics? How clearly does the website answer those questions?

That’s the focus of my objective, AO3: content and lead generation. I’m looking at the information on the pages, including treatment explanations, payment information, and consultation steps. San Gabriel is our current focus. Chino Hills is a future planning area.

What I’ve Done So Far

I compared PhD Orthodontics with Valley Smiles Orthodontics and San Marino Orthodontics for our report. I also prepared and presented that comparison and the content opportunities in our proposal slides.

What I noticed in our review What I want to check next
PhD has early-treatment information, FAQs, provider qualifications, and appointment prompts. How clearly do the pages explain early treatment versus monitoring and what happens at a consultation?
Valley Smiles’ Alhambra page puts treatment information, financing, directions, and a consultation form together. Can someone find PhD’s treatment, payment, and contact information without searching across several pages?
San Marino’s aligner page explains the process, and its Patient Resources page covers insurance and financing. How fully does PhD explain the treatment process and payment options?

These examples come from our report and proposal review. They give me things to check; I haven’t confirmed that those topics are missing from PhD’s website.

My Plan for the Content Audit

I’ll review the relevant service, FAQ, education, financial, provider, location, and consultation pages. For each topic, I’ll save the page URL, review date, and a short excerpt or page location supporting the code.

Code How I’ll use it
0 — Absent An applicable topic isn’t addressed on the reviewed page.
1 — Brief The page mentions the topic but gives little explanation.
2 — Explained The page gives details that help answer the question.

I’ll keep clarity notes separately and check other included pages before calling something a website-wide gap. Inaccessible pages and topics that don’t apply will stay separate from a zero score. Clinical questions will go to the client for checking.

I plan to ask another member to independently review about 20% of the coding records and record agreement before we discuss differences.

What the Survey Would Add

The draft asks what information people need before showing them selected website material. People who confirm reviewing that material then rate its clarity, relevance, coverage, and usefulness. I’ll look at those items separately and keep “cannot judge” apart from neutral answers.

The open questions could show what people still want to know and what might stop them from contacting the practice. The Qualtrics export is a questionnaire draft, not response data. The website material still needs instructor confirmation, so I don’t have survey findings to report yet.

What I’d Like to Produce

I’d like to make a table linking each suggested content change to the reason for it: a question raised in the survey, a topic found in the audit, a competitor example, a review theme, or a captured search topic.

The partial Google Ads search terms can give me topics to check. They don’t show overall search demand or which content led to an inquiry. I’ll use the audit and survey evidence to decide the priorities with the team.
